# Caption architecture

Every Nabbly post caption follows the same four beats, in the same order. The
shape is fixed so writing one is a matter of filling it in rather than starting
from a blank page, and so the account sounds like one person over time instead
of a different writer every week.

Inherits everything in `FEEL.md` §7 (Voice & copy). Where the two disagree,
`FEEL.md` wins.

---

## The shape

**1. The fact.** One sentence. The news, stated flat, first line.

No warm-up, no scene setting, no question. Instagram truncates after roughly
two lines, so this sentence is doing all the work for most people who see it.
It has to survive being the only thing anyone reads.

> Officially partnered with Next Northwest.
> The drafted opening reply is free now.
> Most gigs are gone before you see them.

**2. The substance.** Two or three sentences. What it is and why it matters.

Specifics only. A number that had to be looked up, a named thing, the actual
mechanism. This is the part that earns the tap, and it is the part that is
hardest to fake, which is exactly why it is here.

**3. The human beat.** One sentence a marketing department would cut.

An honest limit, a reason, a small aside, something only the person who built
it would think to say. Optional in principle, but it is the single most
effective thing for not sounding machine written. If a caption feels
frictionless, this beat is missing.

> Your resume is never stored, it only affects the session you are in.
> It is a starting point, not finished work.

**4. The door.** One short instruction, ending in `nabbly.co`.

No hard sell. The reader has already decided by now.

> Free to join at nabbly.co

**Then hashtags**, on their own line, five to eight, varied week to week. Mix
broad and niche. Never inside the sentences.

**Length:** 60 to 110 words before the hashtags. If it runs past that, beat 2
is doing too much.

## Headlines on the image

The same discipline, shorter. **A headline is a plain sentence in which the
product does something.** Subject, verb, object. Not a label, not a noun phrase
wearing a verb, not a category name sitting above a picture.

| Weak | Why | Strong |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Your board learns you. | Noun phrase, and "learns you" is not a thing | The board learns from you. |
| Ranking that learns. | A label. Nothing happens in it | Rate one gig. The board shifts. |
| Scored against your resume. | Passive, no actor | It scores gigs against your resume. |
| On your home screen. | A fragment, a place not an action | It lives on your home screen. |
| Three things that opened up. | Passive, sounds like it happened to us | Three upgrades. All in the free tier. |

The test: can you point at the thing doing the verb? If not, rewrite it.

Two lines, the second in amber. Keep the break where the sentence would
naturally breathe, not wherever it happens to fit.

## Hard rules

Carried over from `FEEL.md`, repeated here because captions break them most:

- **Never advertise sources.** No board names, no source counts, no "we added
  X this month". The board is the product, provenance is plumbing. This rules
  out a whole category of otherwise tempting posts.
- **Vocabulary:** gigs (not jobs or postings) · board (not feed or list) · fields (not
  categories or sources) · "the moment it drops" · "reply first".
- **Dashes only when truly necessary.** Commas and full stops instead.
- Sentence case. No exclamation marks. No emoji.
- **Numbers must be true whenever someone reads the post.** A post outlives the
  day it went up, so state a floor rather than a figure that drifts.
- Never stretch a true statement into a bigger one.

## Tells that read as machine written

Avoid all of these. They are the specific patterns people have learned to
recognise, and one of them is enough to make the rest look generated.

**Structure**

- Rhetorical question openers. "Ever wondered why…", "What if you could…"
- Engagement bait at the end. "What do you think?", "Drop a comment below"
- A closing sentence that restates the caption you just read
- Rule of three in sentence after sentence
- "It's not just X, it's Y" and its cousin "Not X. Y."
- "Whether you're a designer or a developer…"
- Every sentence the same length, so the whole thing hums at one pitch
- Perfectly balanced clauses either side of every comma

**Words**

- unlock, empower, elevate, leverage, streamline, supercharge, transform,
  revolutionise, dive in
- seamless, robust, powerful, cutting edge, game changing, effortless
- "In today's fast paced world", "the reality is", "here's the thing"
- truly, simply, actually, really, as filler

**Formatting**

- Emoji as bullets, or one emoji per line
- Title Case Sentences
- Bold text mid caption for emphasis

## What reads as a person

- **Uneven rhythm.** A long sentence, then a short one. Then a fragment.
- **One specific that had to be checked**, not a round number.
- **Saying what it does not do.** Limits are credibility.
- **Plain verbs.** Put, watch, send, add, drop, reply.
- **Starting a sentence with "So" or "And"** now and then.
- **Leaving one thing unexplained.** Not every noun needs a clause after it.

## Worked example

Beats marked, so the shape is visible:

>

## Before posting

1. Does the first sentence work alone, with everything after it hidden?
2. Is there one specific in it that had to be looked up?
3. Is there a sentence a marketer would have cut? If not, add one.
4. Read it aloud. Do the sentences vary in length?
5. Any word from the banned list above?
6. Any source, board name or count? Cut it.
7. Is every number still true a month from now?
8. Dashes, exclamation marks, emoji: none?
